# Fathom Analytics

## What it is

Fathom is a privacy-focused analytics product aimed at teams that want aggregate insights without tracking individuals. The pricing page is the reference here: a pageview slider sits at the top and the plan comparison reorganizes around the traffic level the visitor picks, instead of forcing them to decode a static table.

## When to open it

- When pricing by usage needs to feel playable rather than tabular: one variable, sliders, immediate feedback.
- When the product promise is privacy, and the pricing page must not undermine it with aggressive upsell UI.
- When a simple, fast dashboard is the selling point and the site design must match that claim.

## Most useful

- The pageview slider focuses the whole comparison on a single decision, which keeps the plan table legible at any scale.
- Plan differences stay about features and team needs, while the price driver (traffic) is handled by the slider, not by fine print.
- The page keeps the conversion path short: pick a traffic level, see the plan, start a trial.
- The visual system stays quiet and confident, matching the privacy positioning instead of chasing growth hacks.

## Using it with agents

- No agent channel is declared on the site at review.
- Analytics dashboards are account-bound; an agent cannot reach them without credentials, so study the public pages instead.

## Watch out for

- Pricing is usage-based and changes with traffic, so check the slider against expected growth before quoting a price.
- No free tier was found at review; the entry point is a paid plan or trial, confirm the current offer on the pricing page.
- The content is proprietary and look-only; do not copy the pricing interaction's copy or illustrations.

## Reusable ideas

- Let one input (traffic, seats, storage) drive the entire pricing comparison, so the table answers the visitor's actual question.
- Keep the pricing page free of upsell clutter when the brand promise is privacy; the quiet page is the proof.
- Show the jump from small to large numbers as a smooth slider range rather than a sparse tier list.
