# Zian Lu

## What it is

Zian Lu's portfolio presents him as a designer focused on typography and brand identity, based in Shanghai, China. The work list includes identity systems, digital experiences, type design and experiments. Project descriptions name collaborators and give a short account of each visual direction.

## When to open it

- When you want to study Chinese design work from a named designer with an explicit Shanghai base.
- When a brand brief needs examples of identity expressed across a site, booking flow or physical touchpoints.
- When you want to explore typography and generative motion as parts of a broader identity practice.

## Most useful

- Start with the [Fuelup identity and booking experience](https://zian.work/). The portfolio describes its high-energy yoga direction through rhythm and music and credits Zian Lu with creative director Lingfeng Liu and motion designer Zhanhong Huang.
- Read the [Motion DNA experiment](https://zian.work/) for an example of expressing motion as a system of tempo, easing, transitions and interaction patterns across components, instead of presenting isolated animations.
- Continue through the identity entries for Celeb Avenue, Solar Nurture and 5Y Capital to compare how the portfolio connects a short brand premise with a specific visual idea and collaborator credits.

## Watch out for

- This is an individual portfolio, not a reusable asset or component library. Project concepts are reference material and do not grant permission to reuse the work.
- Some entries are identity case summaries or experiments rather than standalone live products. Distinguish portfolio evidence from the live behavior of a linked project.
- No reuse licence for portfolio or project assets was stated.

## Reusable ideas

- Give project entries clear role and collaborator credits near the work description.
- Explain a visual identity through the brand premise it serves, then connect that premise to a concrete design device.
- Treat motion as a reusable language with tempo, easing and transition rules that can carry across components.
