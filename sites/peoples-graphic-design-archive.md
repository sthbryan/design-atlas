# People's Graphic Design Archive

## What it is

The People's Graphic Design Archive is a volunteer-run digital archive of graphic design history, co-directed by Brockett Horne, Briar Levit, Louise Sandhaus and Morgan Searcy. Anyone can add an item, as an image, PDF, video, audio file or link, as long as the work is at least ten years old. Items are grouped into collections such as posters, magazines, books and advertisements, and tagged by style and subject.

## When to open it

Open it for period print and packaging references that general web galleries miss, such as magazine spreads, record sleeves, game covers and ads from the late 1990s and early 2000s. The tag pages are useful when a brief names a revival style and you want the original artifacts rather than today's take on them.

## Most useful

- **[Y2K aesthetic tag](https://peoplesgdarchive.org/tag/3474/y2k-aesthetic)**: about 16 items at review, listed by title as magazine issues credited to The Designers Republic, a late-1990s drinks ad, game box art, an iPod advertisement and an entry for the Rave Preservation Project. The images were not visually inspected at review, because the cookie notice covered the page (see Watch out for).
- **[Home](https://peoplesgdarchive.org/)**: sort by item date to walk through a decade, or by most viewed to find well-documented entries.
- **[Legal and privacy](https://peoplesgdarchive.org/legal-privacy)**: the use terms and the takedown contact.

## Using it with agents

No agent channel at review; no API, MCP server or `llms.txt` is documented. Use it in a browser, record the item URL, and describe the composition, type and colour you want to adapt.

## Watch out for

- The archive holds the work for education and research only. Every item belongs to its designer or client, so treat it as look-only.
- Entries are contributed by the public, and the depth of each record, such as the designer, date and printing method, varies from item to item.
- Tags are applied by contributors, so a tag page is a starting point, not a complete survey of a style.
- At review the cookie notice covered the page and offered no reject button. Preferences lets you switch analytics cookies off, but the notice stayed until Accept was pressed, so decide on consent yourself before browsing.

## Reusable ideas

- Require a minimum age for entries, so an archive records history rather than trends.
- Let visitors sort one collection by the date of the work and by the date it was added, as two different stories.
- Put the designer, client, date and medium on every item, so a reference carries its context with it.
