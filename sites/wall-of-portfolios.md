# Wall of Portfolios

## What it is

Wall of Portfolios is a curated wall of designer portfolios, made by Praneeth Jonnala. Each entry is a profile card with the designer's role, country, years of experience, style tag and past employers, linking out to the live portfolio. The site says only about 30% of submissions are accepted and claims more than 45,000 designers as users. At review its style categories counted 820+ minimal, 390+ dark-themed, 300+ interactive, 180+ creative and 90+ modern-layout portfolios (tags overlap). Most featured designers are in India, often at companies such as Swiggy, Zomato, Flipkart, Microsoft or Google.

## When to open it

Open it when you are building or reviewing a product designer's portfolio and want to see how working designers at a given level present case studies, or when you are hiring and want to browse by role and experience.

## Most useful

- **Filters**: company, role (intern to creative director), years of experience, country and "open to work".
- **Portfolio of the Month**: five monthly picks with a short editor's note on why each works.
- **Cases**: a separate wall of case studies grouped by industry (SaaS, fintech, AI, quick commerce, mobility, travel, design systems).
- **Studio**: a feed where designers post work in progress and ask for feedback.
- **Job Tracker**: a free board for tracking your own applications.

## Using it with agents

No API, MCP, llms.txt or export. Use it by hand: pick a few portfolios at your level, note how they structure a case study (problem, role, process, outcome) and how they handle layout and type, and give the agent that outline, not the designers' text or images.

## Watch out for

- Strong regional and product-design skew; few brand, motion or studio portfolios.
- Profiles are self-submitted and then reviewed, so the "top designers" framing is the site's own claim.
- Much of the site (submitting, bookmarking, messaging) is behind a login.

## Reusable ideas

- Tag each portfolio with role and years of experience so people compare with peers, not only with stars.
- Add a short editor's note to monthly picks that says why the portfolio works.
- Separate whole portfolios from single case studies; people look for them at different moments.
