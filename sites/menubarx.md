# MenubarX

## What it is

MenubarX puts a browser in the menu bar: you pin sites or PWAs and open them in a small panel, with per-site user agents so a page can be made to behave like a phone, a tablet or a desktop, and a detach action when the panel gets too small. The page itself is almost aggressively minimal — an app icon, one sentence, a 4.9 rating with 15,000 reviews, and a feature list in small type at the bottom.

## When to open it

Open it when you are designing a store-style landing page for a small app and want to see how little a page can contain while still selling, or when you need a menu-bar panel pattern with resizing and pinned state.

## Most useful

- **The 4.9 with 15,000 ratings** placed directly under the title, where it does the most work.
- **Feature list as a dense stack** (web apps, ad blocking, bookmarks, iCloud, pinned, detach, resize) rather than illustrated cards.
- **User-agent switching** called out as a differentiator, which is the detail that makes a menu-bar browser usable.
- **Resize and detach actions** on the panel, treated as first-class rather than hidden.
- **Footer as an index**: privacy, terms, press kit, FAQ, old versions in one line.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was found at review. Nothing here is machine-readable beyond the page text, and the app is a macOS client behind a purchase.

## Watch out for

- The page has no screenshots of the panel in use, so a reader has to install it to see the interaction.
- Pro pricing is not shown; "Buy Pro" is the only route.
- A cross-sell to the maker's other app sits above the fold.
- Ratings are a single aggregate without a date or store split.
- Sites that refuse to render in a small panel are the main practical failure, and the page does not mention it.

## Reusable ideas

- Put the rating under the product name, before the description.
- List capabilities as a compact stack when the audience already understands the category.
- Treat detach and resize as features worth naming; they are what makes a panel usable.
- Use the footer as a complete index of the small pages.
