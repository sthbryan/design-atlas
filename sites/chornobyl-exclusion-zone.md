# Chornobyl Exclusion Zone — Banda

## What it is

Banda's identity case for Ukraine's Chornobyl Exclusion Zone, commissioned with the Ministry of Environmental Protection and Natural Resources of Ukraine and the State Agency on Exclusion Zone Management. Banda's home says the agency was founded in Kyiv and lists offices in Kyiv and Los Angeles.

## When to open it

Open the [case study](https://www.banda.agency/projects/chornobyl) when a visual identity needs to encode a change over time, or when a static logo would flatten a project's central idea.

## Most useful

- The first screen is a black-and-white archival photograph under a narrow, oversized title bar. As the page unfolds, large section titles and generous white space give each part of the story room.
- The mark is based on the fourth reactor's silhouette. The case lays out dated versions from 1986 onward, showing the shape changing and disappearing over time toward the plant's planned 2064 decommissioning.
- The annual transition happens on April 26, the disaster's anniversary. That date ties the identity's changing state to the subject's history.
- The project page also shows related applications and motion through embedded videos; Banda's [home](https://www.banda.agency/) places this public-sector case among campaign and commercial work.

## Watch out for

- The site's storytelling and archival imagery are tied to a real disaster and its continuing effects; retain that context when discussing or adapting the design.
- Treat the logo, photos, videos and copy as look-only. No reuse licence was stated.
- The intended 2064 endpoint is described in the case as a future milestone; it is part of the concept, not evidence of a completed future state.

## Reusable ideas

- Make a logo's changing state carry the story when time itself is central to the subject.
- Use a meaningful anniversary as the update date so the system's rhythm reinforces its history.
- Show a sequence of dated marks together to make a gradual transformation legible at a glance.
