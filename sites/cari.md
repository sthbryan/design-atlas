# CARI

## What it is

CARI, the Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute, is a community archive that names and dates the visual styles of consumer products, advertising and interfaces from the 1970s on. Each aesthetic has its own page with a period of relevance, the people credited with researching and naming it, related aesthetics and a gallery of period images. The index can be filtered by keyword and sorted by earliest known example, end of popularity or decade. The site's own pages are plain: a black circle logo, white ground and a grid of rounded image tiles, so the gallery images carry the look.

## When to open it

Open it when a brief names a revival look, such as Y2K, Neo-Y2K or Frutiger Aero, and you need to know what the original period actually used before deciding what to adapt. It is a way to get past the stock version of a named style to the materials, dates and motifs behind it.

## Most useful

- **[Index of aesthetics](https://cari.institute/aesthetics)**: sort by decade or earliest example to see which looks overlap in time.
- **[Y2K aesthetic](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/y2k-aesthetic)**: shiny synthetic materials, silver, gradients and blobby consumer electronics, with the researchers who defined the term credited on the page. The gallery seen at review is a grid of square period images: a CGI tube interior, wet purple lips, a metallic beauty ad, a blue software progress dialog and a console menu of glossy blue orbs. Study how icy blue and violet, wet highlights and interface chrome recur across unrelated products, rather than any one picture.
- **[Neo-Y2K](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/neo-y2k)**: the late-2010s revival, with saturated colour and influence from East Asian graphic design; useful for telling a revival from the original.
- **[Frutiger Aero](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/frutiger-aero)**: glassy and transparent materials, glossy skeuomorphic interfaces, humanist sans type, aurora and bokeh photography, dated to roughly the mid-2000s to early 2010s. A period reference for glass briefs.
- **[Glossary](https://cari.institute/glossary)** and **[attribution guidelines](https://cari.institute/attribution-guidelines)**: the vocabulary and the crediting rules the site asks for.

## Using it with agents

No agent channel at review: `/llms.txt` returned 404, and no API or MCP server is documented. Open individual aesthetic pages in a browser and record the page URL with what you saw. Describe the moves in your own words rather than pasting the page text.

## Watch out for

- The gallery images are other people's advertising, packaging and interfaces. Treat every image as look-only, whatever the page's research value.
- Some period fields disagreed within one page at review, so treat the dates as approximate.
- The site is community research. Names such as Frutiger Aero are recent labels applied after the fact, not terms the original designers used.

## Reusable ideas

- Give each style entry a start date, an end date and named sources, so a style is a dated record rather than a mood.
- Link each aesthetic to its neighbours in time, so a reader can see what it grew from and what replaced it.
- Credit the person who researched or named a category next to the category itself.
