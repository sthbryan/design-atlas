# Showit

## What it is

Showit is a drag-and-drop website builder aimed at photographers, coaches and other solo creatives, and its page is built almost entirely from social proof. A band of adjectives runs through the hero ("professional, stunning, clean, beautiful, creative, modern"), then a row of named practitioners, then a long testimonial wall where each quote keeps its author's name and business. The design argument arrives later as a checklist of things you can move — fine-tune layouts, adjust images, modify icons, rotate elements, play with animations — written in the first person and without screenshots of the editor.

## When to open it

Open it when you are designing a page for a tool sold on trust rather than features, and want to see how far a testimonial layout can carry a landing page. It is also a decent example of a checklist standing in for a feature comparison.

## Most useful

- **Testimonial wall with attribution**: names, professions and screenshots of real voices rather than logos.
- **Adjective band through the hero**, which sets a tone before any claim is made.
- **Action checklist** instead of a feature matrix: verbs the user does, not capabilities the tool has.
- **Template gallery split into free and premium**, which lets a visitor judge the visual ceiling before paying.
- **WordPress blog plans**, so the blog half of a site keeps a familiar editor.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was found at review, and the builder is a desktop app for design plus a hosted site. An agent cannot drive it. The useful extraction is the page pattern: hero adjectives, proof row, testimonial wall, action checklist.

## Watch out for

- The site's own layout is unconventional and dense, with several competing claims in the first screen; copying it wholesale would produce a shouty page.
- Pricing is presented in three tiers where the difference is mostly the blog, which the plan names do not make obvious.
- The WordPress blog is a separate product bundled in; the design freedom does not extend to the blog.
- Testimonials are self-selected and include well-known names in its niche, which is proof of community, not of design quality.
- No editor screenshots on the pages reviewed, so the design control being sold has to be taken on trust.

## Reusable ideas

- Carry a short adjective band through the hero to set tone before the pitch.
- Replace the feature matrix with a checklist of verbs the user performs.
- Keep names and professions on testimonials; a wall of anonymous quotes reads as filler.
- Let people browse the template gallery, free and paid, before they see pricing.
