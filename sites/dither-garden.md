# Dither Garden

## What it is

Dither Garden is a browser editor for turning an image into patterned, limited-colour art. At review, the page listed 15 algorithms, including Floyd–Steinberg, Atkinson, Stucki, Bayer, blue noise, Riemersma and ASCII; four colour modes; and PNG, JPG, WebP and TXT exports. The site labels September 2025 as its latest update.

## When to open it

- When you want to compare familiar diffusion, ordered and noise-based dithering on an image.
- When you need a quick monochrome, duotone, tritone or RGB variation without installing an editor.
- When a plain-text export is useful alongside raster image files.

## Most useful

- **Algorithm list**: the home page names classic diffusion methods, Bayer, threshold, blue noise, Riemersma and ASCII art.
- **Colour modes**: choose monochrome, duotone, tritone or RGB.
- **Export choices**: save PNG, JPG, WebP or TXT after processing.
- **Visual direction**: the live page pairs a narrow white instruction and upload column with a broad dark preview area. Monospaced labels, dotted dividers and pixelated horse studies make the effect itself part of the page's identity.

## Using it with agents

There is no published MCP, API, CLI, `llms.txt` or agent guide. Use the browser editor directly and bring the exported file into the project.

## Watch out for

- The page's latest-update label is September 2025; algorithm and export counts are as stated on the live page at review.
- Its cookie notice describes cookie use, but the page does not link a privacy policy from the visible interface.
- The site does not state a licence for its code or generated files. Treat the site and its sample artwork as look-only unless the owner clarifies reuse rights.

## Reusable ideas

- Put the upload instructions next to a large, persistent result area so the image remains the focus while the workflow stays legible.
- Show the available algorithms, colour modes and export formats on the landing page; this makes the tool's range clear before a file is selected.
- Repeat the treatment in sample artwork so visitors can see the visual effect without first supplying an image.
