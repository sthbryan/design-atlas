# Wonderlist

## What it is

Wonderlist sells a fixed catalogue of website designs drawn in Figma and sold for development. At review the catalogue was 127 designs and 1,137 unique pages, counted across categories it labels SaaS (53 designs), Portfolio (30), Agency (21) and Other (23), with the last group covering architecture, dental, beauty, finance, travel, real estate and similar briefs. Each design is listed by category and number, such as SaaS 01 through SaaS 10, so buyers can scan a range rather than a random set. It is positioned as a production shortcut: pick a layout, take the file, build the site.

## When to open it

Open it when you need a conventional marketing-site or portfolio structure quickly — pricing block, feature grid, logo row, testimonial, contact — and want to see the complete page rather than assemble it. It is a starting-point library, not an inspiration feed: every entry is one more variation on the same commercial patterns.

## Most useful

- **Complete page compositions**: the 1,137 page designs include inner pages, not just homepages, which is where most template libraries are thin.
- **Vertical coverage**: industry briefs outside tech — dental, beauty, construction, real estate — are unusual in this kind of pack.
- **Fixed packages**: Lite (25 designs, 212 pages), Pro (127 designs) and Business (three seats plus monthly additions) make the trade-off explicit.
- **Included extras**: a font pack ships with each design, and the site states the icons, logos and mockups used in its designs are free to reuse.
- **Single payment**: the licence is bought once rather than subscribed, which suits a one-off client build.

## Using it with agents

No MCP, API, `llms.txt` or public sitemap was found, and the catalogue pages carry no structured data an agent can rely on. An agent can open a category page and read the design names, but it cannot inspect files, which are delivered after purchase. In practice a person picks the design and hands the agent the exported layout as a brief.

## Watch out for

- The FAQ says designs are delivered as PNG and SVG, while the sales copy promises editable Figma files and a Figma preview. Confirm the format before paying.
- A countdown timer and a 50% code sit on the page at review; treat the pricing as promotional rather than fixed.
- Lite and Pro are single-user licences, so a team build needs the Business tier.
- No terms or licence page was found at review. The stated MIT licence covers images and fonts, not the layouts, so ask for the reuse terms in writing.
- Reviews and customer counts ("4.9/5 from 400+", "11k+ customers") are the seller's own figures.

## Reusable ideas

- Name entries by category and number so a big catalogue stays scannable, the way a stock library does.
- Sell complete page sets, including checkout, blog and contact pages, instead of homepages alone.
- Cover unglamorous verticals; dental or construction briefs are often where a team runs out of references.
- Publish which assets inside a pack are freely licensed, separate from the pack's own licence.
