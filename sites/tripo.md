# Tripo

## What it is

Tripo generates 3D models from text or images, and its pricing page is unusually concrete about the unit: each plan lists credits, an approximate model count and a per-model price — 3,000 credits for about 200 models, which the page works out at $0.16 each, and 25,000 credits for about 1,660 at $0.09. The tiers also gate the things a designer cares about: private versus public models, commercial use, 8K textures, retopology, smart segmentation and a bridge into DCC tools.

## When to open it

Open it when you need a placeholder or a hero asset that would take longer to model than to generate, and you need to know what a usable mesh costs before committing. The plan comparison is also a reference for pricing a generation service in units people can reason about.

## Most useful

- **Per-model price on every tier**, derived from credits rather than left as arithmetic for the buyer.
- **Concurrency and queue priority** stated as part of the plan, which is what actually decides whether a batch is practical.
- **Public versus private models** as the real dividing line between free and paid.
- **Production tools named**: retopology, smart segmentation, multi-view to 3D, DCC bridge for batch export.
- **Credit packs with bonus credits**, for burst work without changing tier.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was documented on the pages read. Generation runs in the web app or studio, so an agent cannot produce a mesh here; the page is a pricing and capability reference.

## Watch out for

- The free tier's models are public and non-commercial, which rules out client work.
- Model quality and retopology results are not shown on the pricing page; "game-ready in 2s" is a marketing claim.
- Per-model prices assume the approximations hold, and regeneration eats credits.
- The Team plan's feature list ends with "more team features coming soon".
- Output licence terms are spread across plans rather than stated as a single grant.

## Reusable ideas

- Quote the unit price your credits actually buy, on the plan card.
- Make the private/public distinction the free-tier boundary; it is easy to explain.
- Show concurrency, because waiting is the cost nobody prices.
- Offer top-up packs so a one-off batch does not force an upgrade.
