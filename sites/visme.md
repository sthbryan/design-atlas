# Visme

## What it is

Visme's home page solves a specific layout problem: one tool that produces eight different kinds of artefact. The answer is a horizontal band of output types — presentations, docs, data visualisations, whiteboards, infographics, social graphics, forms and surveys, videos — each with a one-line job and a "Try it free" link, so the reader picks a lane instead of reading a feature list. The band repeats the same anatomy eight times, which is monotonous but very scannable, and it keeps the page shallow before the case studies.

## When to open it

Open it when a product has many output formats and you need a way to present them without a mega-menu, or when you want a reference for how to place customer stories with named roles beneath a feature grid.

## Most useful

- **Eight equal output cards**: same height, same label position, same single action, which makes comparison trivial.
- **Job lines rather than feature bullets**: each card names what the reader would make.
- **Case studies with role and organisation** (partnership strategy at a football club, evaluation director at a university), which is stronger proof than logos.
- **Data visualisation treated as a format of its own**, not as a chart widget inside presentations.
- **A stated AI route** from a prompt to a finished design, positioned as one entry among many rather than the headline.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was found at review, and designs are made in the web app. An agent can read the page's structure, but the templates and charts are behind an account.

## Watch out for

- Eight equal cards give no guidance on where to start; everything looks equally important.
- Pricing is not shown on the pages read, and the free tier's limits are unclear.
- The page claims 35M+ users without defining the period.
- Case studies sit far below the format band, so the proof arrives after the choice.
- The design is competent rather than distinctive — a useful structural reference, not a style one.

## Reusable ideas

- Lay out many output formats as an equal-weight row with one line of job text each.
- Give every card the same single call to action so the row stays quiet.
- Put role and organisation on testimonials instead of company logos alone.
- Keep the AI claim as one format among the others rather than the whole pitch.
