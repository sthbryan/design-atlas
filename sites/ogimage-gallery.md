# OGimage.gallery

## What it is

OGimage.gallery collects Open Graph images, the landscape card a link shows when it is shared. Each entry links out to the site it came from, and the collection is filtered by category — animated, app, arts and culture, creative, dynamic, finance, food, health, portfolios, resources, SaaS, stores — with a second index organised by site. The wall is made of the images themselves at their real aspect ratio, so the page reads as a grid of link previews rather than of thumbnails.

## When to open it

- You are designing a share card and want to see what well-funded teams actually put in a 1.91:1 frame.
- You need to argue that a plain logo on a coloured background is the default everyone uses, and show the alternatives.
- You want to check whether a static card is enough or whether the animated ones earn their extra weight.

## Most useful

- **The card at real proportion**: seeing the previews side by side makes the composition constraints obvious — one line of type, a product shot, a single accent, or a full-bleed illustration with the title kept clear of the corners that platforms crop.
- **Categories that map to presentation problems**: "animated", "dynamic" and "creative" are separated from "app", "SaaS" and "stores", which is a useful split between cards that show the product and cards that show a mood.
- **By-site index**: a list of around ninety sites at review, from Apple, OpenAI, Vercel and Stripe to smaller studios and tools such as Linear-adjacent products, Shopify and Ramp. It doubles as a shortlist of teams whose sharing looks considered.
- **Followers to the source**: each card opens the live site, so you can check how the card relates to the page it represents.

## Using it with agents

No agent channel at review: no `llms.txt`, no API. The images are third-party assets, so an agent should not download or collage them. Point an agent at the source sites instead, and describe the composition you want in words.

## Watch out for

- Every image belongs to the site that commissioned it. The gallery states no licence, so this is reference, not material.
- Cards go stale: a page redesign does not update the preview the gallery captured, so check the live site before citing one as current.
- Preview cards are generated by the platform from metadata, and some entries may be hand-made images set as `og:image`. The difference matters when you copy an idea.
- A gallery of strong cards selects for visual ambition, not for click-through. No performance data is offered, and a beautiful card is not evidence that it works.
- The site is a small side project with light maintenance. Do not build a workflow on a URL that might move.

## Reusable ideas

- Judge a share card at its real size, next to others; the frame is the constraint.
- Separate cards that show the product from cards that set a mood, and design each on purpose.
- Keep a preview of the card inside the product's own settings, so the person writing the page sees what will be shared.
- Treat the OG image as part of the page's art direction rather than a stamp of the logo.
