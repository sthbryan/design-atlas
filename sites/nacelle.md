# Nacelle

## What it is

Nacelle is a neo-grotesque sans-serif by DOT COLON, presented on a Japanese foundry page. The specimen gives the family one enormous word sample beside a narrow information column with the style list, download and licence.

## When to open it

Open the [Nacelle specimen](https://dotcolon.net/fonts/nacelle/) when studying restrained type-first composition, a two-column specimen layout or an open font with a broad weight range.

## Most useful

- **Split composition** — a slim left panel holds the name, version, weights, download and licence, while the right side gives most of the viewport to the word “Nacelle” in huge black type.
- **Family overview** — the page names Thin, UltraLight, Light, Regular, SemiBold, Bold, Heavy, Black and Italic styles.
- **Clear reuse terms** — the page links SIL Open Font License 1.1 and states in Japanese that web, print and logo use, modification and redistribution are permitted.
- The accompanying Japanese description positions Nacelle as a more natural revision of Aileron, retaining its goal of readable and distinguishable UI lettering.

## Using it with agents

There is no published MCP, API, CLI or agent guide. The specimen exposes the font name, style list, downloadable archive and licence in ordinary page content, so a browser can retrieve those facts directly. The page itself does not provide an interactive text tester.

## Watch out for

- The live specimen is intentionally sparse and currently offers no visible controls for changing the sample text or style.
- The site interface mixes Japanese and English. Browser translation is available, but the English information is already enough to identify the family, styles and licence.
- The OFL applies to the font files, not the site's layout or type specimen artwork.

## Reusable ideas

- Give the type sample most of the viewport and keep download and licensing information in a stable narrow rail.
- Let the font name itself serve as the main visual sample instead of adding decorative imagery.
- Pair an explicit style list with a short design rationale, so the family is useful even without a live tester.
