# Raycast

## What it is

Raycast is a keyboard-driven launcher for macOS, and its site is a study in product voice: the whole page speaks the product's language, keyboard-first and precise, so a utility feels like a coherent world instead of a feature checklist.

## When to open it

- When a productivity tool's landing page must teach the core interaction (the keyboard flow) in the hero.
- When brand voice is the differentiator and the site copy must demonstrate it, not just claim it.
- When extensions are the growth path and the site must show them as a living catalog, not a download list.

## Most useful

- Keyboard-first visuals: the product is shown the way it is used, fingers on keys, commands as the unit of design.
- A strong, consistent voice across headlines, sections and footer, so the tool's personality is the whole pitch.
- The extension catalog presented as a world to explore, which matches how users actually adopt the tool.
- The site and the product share a visual system, so the landing page reads as a tour of the app.

## Using it with agents

- No agent channel is declared on the marketing site at review; the app itself is the agent surface, behind an account.
- The public pages and docs are the agent reference; automation beyond the app needs an account.

## Watch out for

- Plan limits at review are stated on the pricing page; confirm live before budgeting, especially for teams and AI features.
- The product is macOS-first at review; check platform support before recommending it to non-mac users.
- All content is proprietary and look-only; study the voice and structure, do not reuse the copy or visuals.

## Reusable ideas

- Show the tool the way it is used (keyboard flows, command lists) instead of static screenshots of windows.
- Let one voice carry the whole landing page when personality is the differentiator; consistency is the feature.
- Present an extension or integration catalog as a browsable world with one-command actions, not as a directory table.
