# BaseFrame

## What it is

BaseFrame is Pratik Singh's product-design portfolio, focused on logistics, operations and other complex software. The homepage combines selected work with case-study links, service descriptions and a public pricing section. Its portfolio is the visual source; the projects shown belong to clients and should be treated as separate products.

## When to open it

- When studying how a portfolio explains complex software work through case studies.
- When looking for operational dashboard and logistics interface examples across desktop and mobile.
- When comparing how a solo design practice presents services, client work and engagement options.

## Most useful

Open the [Savari case study](https://www.baseframe.design/case-studies/savari) for logistics workflows, product hierarchy and mobile adaptations, or the [Shadow Interviewer case study](https://www.baseframe.design/case-studies/shadow-interviewer) for a large recruiting product. These detailed project pages show client work; the portfolio's own visual design is the restrained, typographic frame around those examples. The homepage's [services and pricing](https://www.baseframe.design/#pricing) are useful references for making a design engagement legible.

## Using it with agents

No MCP, API, CLI, `llms.txt` or agent-specific guide was found on the reviewed pages. Use the public case studies as visual references and verify any underlying client product separately.

## Watch out for

- Case studies are selected work presented by the designer, not independent usability evaluations.
- Browsing the portfolio is free; hiring the designer is a separate paid service. The homepage lists a four-week sprint at $6,000 and an ongoing partnership at $7,500/month at review.
- Distinguish BaseFrame's own portfolio layout from the client product interfaces shown inside its case studies.
- No reuse license was stated for the portfolio or case-study visuals; use them for reference only.

## Reusable ideas

- Organize a portfolio around concrete client problems and outcomes, then link each one to a deeper case study.
- Explain the context and information hierarchy behind dense operational screens.
- Show desktop and mobile as separate design contexts rather than shrinking one layout into the other.
