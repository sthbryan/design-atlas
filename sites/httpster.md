# Httpster

## What it is

Httpster is a long-running gallery of website references. At review it listed 3,116 featured sites. Its white home page has a rounded grey Styles/Types bar, a large sans-serif heading and a three-column grid of large site captures. The listings link out to the original websites.

The [Styles filter](https://httpster.net/) includes visual directions such as Brutalist, Minimal, Photographic, Typographic, Responsive, Interactive and Unusual Layout. Types include areas such as Ecommerce, Architecture & Industrial Design, Art & Photography, and Design & Illustration. These two axes help you search by either how a site looks or what it is for.

The live [Drams site](https://drams.framer.website/) is one useful example: its minimal navigation sits above a large orange panel on white, with an oversized grey headline. Study that live page separately from the gallery's own captures and grid.

## When to open it

Use Httpster when you want to browse a broad range of websites by both visual treatment and subject, especially for typography, image-led layouts, unusual navigation or scroll behavior. Follow a capture to the original when you need to check the current design or interaction.

## Most useful

- Filter by a visible trait such as Photographic, Brutalist, Typographic, Grid or Scrolling Behaviour.
- Switch to Types when you want a more focused set, such as Ecommerce, Architecture & Industrial Design, or Art & Photography.
- Compare the large captures in the three-column grid to spot differences in page composition before opening individual references.
- Use the live link beside a listing to check the current site and its behavior; the archive image alone is a point-in-time reference.

## Using it with agents

No MCP, API, CLI or agent-specific channel was visible on the reviewed pages. An agent can browse the style and type filters, shortlist a few captures, and inspect the linked live sites in a browser. Record observations from the archive separately from changes visible on the current site.

## Watch out for

- The archive spans many years, so some captures and links may describe older work. Check the publication date when it is shown and confirm that the linked site still loads.
- A capture does not show every responsive state or interaction. Open the live source before making claims about behavior.
- The gallery does not state a general licence for its captures. Use them for visual study and check the maker's terms before copying code or assets.

## Reusable ideas

- Separate visual-style filters from industry or site-type filters, allowing visitors to browse by either aesthetic or context.
- Place large capture cards immediately below the filters so people can judge visual direction before opening an entry.
- Keep a direct live-site link with each capture so a static reference can lead to a current example.
- Drams demonstrates how a large, saturated orange panel can carry the focal point while white space, minimal navigation and restrained grey type frame it.
