# Nike Why Do It?

## What it is

Nike’s September 2025 campaign announcement for “Why Do It?”, a youth-oriented reworking of the brand’s sports campaign language. The page combines a large athlete image, a campaign film and a photo-led asset gallery.

## When to open it

Use the [campaign release](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-why-do-it-campaign) as a historical example of a sports campaign told through moving image and photography. It documents one 2025 campaign, not a current Nike identity guide.

## Most useful

- The newsroom page begins with a near-black field and a very large white headline, with the date and sharing control set apart on the left. The change from that dark opening to a white editorial body gives the campaign material a clean entrance.
- In the opening content, a wide athlete photograph sits beside a narrow “What to know” text column. The image uses natural light and open sky; the page’s broad crop lets the person’s expression and movement carry the frame.
- The release offers a campaign film and a downloadable asset package. Use the film to study the campaign’s pacing and transitions, and the gallery to compare how athlete portraits carry across the campaign. Motion is a core part of the reference, though individual edits may vary by placement.

## Watch out for

- This page is a campaign announcement rather than a system specification. Its black header and white editorial layout belong to Nike’s newsroom shell; distinguish those from the campaign’s own photography and film direction.
- Nike’s page links to its terms and privacy policy. No licence for the campaign photography or film is stated in the release; treat both as look-only and do not reuse Nike marks.

## Reusable ideas

- Let an opening headline and a single strong image establish a campaign before introducing supporting details.
- Keep editorial metadata and summary content legible beside large campaign media.
- Use a film and a still-image sequence together to test whether a campaign’s visual cues survive both motion and a single-frame crop.
