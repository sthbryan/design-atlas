# Data Viz Project

## What it is

A visual catalogue of chart and diagram forms, maintained by data-visualization agency Ferdio. The main index shows each form as a small, consistently styled illustration. Its companion [1 Dataset, 100 Visualizations](https://100.datavizproject.com/) applies one World Heritage Sites dataset to many chart shapes.

## When to open it

- When deciding which visual form could express a dataset or relationship before choosing a charting library.
- When a familiar chart feels too limited and you want to compare alternatives such as alluvial, treemap, radial, map or distribution forms.
- When you need a quick visual vocabulary for a discussion about chart shape.

## Most useful

- Start on [All visualizations](https://datavizproject.com/) and use **Family**, **Input**, **Function** and **Shape** to narrow the catalogue. Compare the illustrations in the grid, not only their names: the examples use restrained red, navy and grey on white, so each form is easy to distinguish.
- Open the [100 visualizations study](https://100.datavizproject.com/) and compare how the same small table becomes different compositions. The first view is a treemap; the numbered links lead to individual treatments. Look at what each form makes prominent and what it hides.
- The site is a reference for form selection and visual structure. It does not supply a chart implementation or interactive production component.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP, API or CLI was found. Give an agent a specific chart-form page or the 100-visualization study and ask it to compare visual encodings for your data; verify the result against the live examples yourself.

## Watch out for

- The catalogue illustrations are explanatory examples, not a production chart library. Their sample palette, labels and composition are not a visual system to copy wholesale.
- No reuse licence was stated for the diagrams or study. Treat them as look-only, and do not reproduce the agency's artwork.
- A chart's shape alone does not establish that it is the clearest or most accessible choice for a particular dataset.

## Reusable ideas

- Give every chart form the same compact card treatment so people can compare shapes without learning a new page layout.
- Filter a large reference catalogue by multiple dimensions such as input, purpose and geometry.
- Hold the dataset steady while changing the encoding to make the design consequences visible.
