# Google Evolving Identity

## What it is

Google Design's 2015 case study documenting a logo update and its use across digital products. The page shows the multicolour wordmark, the compact G and four animated dots, then explains how the pieces fit into a broader identity system.

## When to open it

Open it to study how a familiar wordmark can be distilled into smaller marks and an interaction cue, or how an identity case study can move from sketches to rules and product examples.

## Most useful

- The opening pairs a large logo image with a restrained dark frame, then shifts into a simple white editorial column.
- The “Elements” visual compares the wordmark, dots and compact G side by side, making the system easy to scan.
- Later examples connect geometry, spacing, color and motion to real product contexts. The motion section shows several states rather than one decorative loop.

## Using it with agents

No MCP, CLI, public API, agent skill or `llms.txt` is published here; the checked `llms.txt` path returns a not-found page. An agent can read the article in a browser and describe the visible marks and rules, while treating Google artwork as look-only.

## Watch out for

- This is a historical 2015 design account. Google's brand and products have evolved since publication.
- Google does not state a general reuse licence for the article's visual assets. Treat the logo, type specimens and images as protected material.
- Product Sans is described as a custom typeface in the article; do not assume it is available for general use.

## Reusable ideas

- Build a small-space mark from the same geometry and color logic as the full wordmark.
- Give motion a semantic role, such as listening or confirming, and use a consistent path and timing system.
- Show a design system through both its core parts and examples of how they work together in product.
