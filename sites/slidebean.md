# Slidebean

## What it is

Slidebean sells two things on one page and the layout says so: an AI deck builder on the left of the pitch and a design agency on the right. The page opens with a sign-up block that names an audience rather than a feature ("helping founders master pitch decks & financials"), then a three-line checkmark list of numbers — raised, founders using the formulas, startups a month — before splitting into the software route and the service route. A Trustpilot count sits above the fold.

## When to open it

Open it when you are deciding how to present a product that also sells a service, and you need a pattern for routing two kinds of buyer without two landing pages. The checkmark proof list is the transferable piece.

## Most useful

- **Two-route structure**: software for people who will build it, agency for people who will not, both explained with the same visual weight.
- **Proof as a short list**, three items, each a number and a noun, in the first screen.
- **Named outcomes in testimonials**: company, amount raised, role — the only kind of quote that means anything in this niche.
- **Case-study numbers repeated as a band** ($500M+ raised, 500+ clients) rather than scattered.
- **Design handled by default**: the software's stated promise is that layout is automatic once content exists, which is the right framing for founders.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was found at review. Deck building happens in an account and the agency is a human service, so an agent can only read the page as a copy and layout reference.

## Watch out for

- Funding totals and client counts are self-reported and presented without dates.
- Prices are not published; both routes end in a form or a call.
- The sign-up block appears before any explanation of what the tool does.
- Trustpilot widget and agency case studies mix two different kinds of evidence.
- The design language is safe and B2B-generic; copy the routing logic, not the look.

## Reusable ideas

- Put the audience in the first line, not the product name.
- Give a service and a self-serve tool equal visual weight when both are real offers.
- Attach amounts to testimonials; a quote without an outcome is decoration.
- Repeat the flagship number as a band further down the page.
