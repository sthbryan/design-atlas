# Page Flows

## What it is

Page Flows is a reference archive of recorded web and mobile product journeys, individual screens and interface elements. A flow page pairs a video with timestamped annotations and a screen sequence, making it possible to study both the appearance of a screen and the transition that leads to it. Free previews are available; the full library and its search tools require a paid subscription.

## When to open it

Use Page Flows when designing onboarding, checkout, account setup, search or another multi-step experience. It is useful for seeing the order of screens, where friction appears and how small interaction states fit into the complete journey.

## Most useful

- The iOS collection starts with product examples and also groups screens, UI elements and full flows into separate browsing paths.
- A flow detail keeps the video central, places the list of other flows in a left column and shows timestamped chapter annotations beside the recording.
- The inspected Robinhood onboarding page lists 43 screens and marks steps such as app listing, splash screen, sign-up, identity checks, bank connection and completion.
- The site uses a restrained charcoal canvas, large white headings and a bright yellow accent for primary trial actions; the product screens remain the visual focus.

## Using it with agents

The site does not advertise a public agent interface. An agent can use the open preview as a visual reference, but some steps and recordings are hidden behind account or trial access. Share the specific flow URL with an agent and describe the interaction stage to study.

## Watch out for

- The inspected Robinhood recording is labeled November 2023; check the version selector before treating an older flow as current.
- Some recordings show only a preview until sign-in or subscription, and the paid plan is billed quarterly or yearly.
- The screenshots depict third-party apps and the archive is copyrighted; treat the material as reference rather than reusable assets.

## Reusable ideas

- Make a long flow scannable with timestamped labels for each meaningful transition.
- Keep the screen sequence close to the video so viewers can relate individual captures to motion.
- Group related flows for one product, then provide a cross-product route for comparing the same task.
