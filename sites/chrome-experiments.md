# Chrome Experiments

## What it is

An archive of browser projects by independent creators, organized into collections such as Chrome + WebGL and Chrome + Music. Collection pages pair a short description and preview with a link to the project's own site, so the archive is a way into the experiments rather than a host for most of them.

## When to open it

Open the [WebGL collection](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/collection/chrome?tag=WebGL) when you need a concrete example of a browser technology used as an expressive visual experience: procedural scenes, data-driven globes, music visualizations or interactive 3D art.

## Most useful

- [Biomes](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/biomes) launches a live full-screen terrain study: low-poly rolling ridges fill a black canvas in orange, green and pale pink, with a compact right-side panel for wave dimensions, height, three color stops, randomizing and downloading. The broad colored bands make the terrain legible as a composed scene instead of a wireframe demo.
- The archive also links [Surface Floater](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/surface-floater), [WebGL Globe](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/chrome/globe), [Tendrils](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/tendrils) and [Flame](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/flame); their launch pages were not opened in this review, so their rendered compositions are unverified here.

Biomes is the only demo in this entry with a verified live rendering. Treat the other archive records as leads until their current launch links have been checked.

## Using it with agents

There is no MCP, API, CLI or agent guide. Give an agent a specific experiment page and its live demo link, then ask it to describe the visible composition and interaction. The project may live on another domain and may have its own documentation and licence.

## Watch out for

- This is a long-running archive. Some launch links lead to old projects that may no longer load in a current browser; verify the demo before relying on it.
- The archive does not give a blanket licence for its entries. A visible demo is inspiration, not permission to copy its artwork or source.
- Many experiments expect WebGL, audio or camera input. Check browser support and permissions before using them as references for a constrained device.

## Reusable ideas

- Pair a technology tag with direct launch links, so a gallery visitor can move from a short concept to the original interactive work.
- Let a small set of curated examples show different expressive uses of one technology, instead of making the technology label itself do the explaining.
- When a demo is generative, expose an obvious way to randomize or tune the result so visitors can see the range of the system.
