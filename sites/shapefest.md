# Shapefest

## What it is

Shapefest is a library of rendered 3D illustrations made as a visual system: the shapes share camera, lighting, and material treatment across each collection. Its material collections cover clay, plastic, glass, and metal; separate packs group geometry, hands, letters, objects, and isometric illustrations.

## When to open it

Open the [Neon Glass collection](https://shapefest.com/collections/neon-glass) to study the blue-violet translucent forms: a cone, connected atom-like spheres, nested blocks, stacked discs, and a spring rendered against black. This is a useful reference for hero art, feature illustrations, or a dark product surface that needs one consistent 3D vocabulary. For softer, more neutral scenes, compare the [Clay Design packs](https://shapefest.com/collections/design-illustrations).

## Most useful

- Material collections let you keep a consistent finish while changing the subject, from simple spheres and blocks to complete object families.
- Floating and isometric compositions provide different camera languages for hero artwork, feature cards, and compact illustrations.
- Individual pack pages show object counts, camera angles, resolution, and licence; free packs are available as 512px images, with 3,000px downloads sold separately or through All Access.

## Using it with agents

There is no published MCP, API, CLI, or agent skill for the library. An agent can still use the stable collection or pack links as a visual brief: specify the material, camera, background, and intended scale, then select a pack and check its licence before using the downloaded file.

## Watch out for

- The rendered images are raster PNGs, so they do not provide a live 3D model for changing the camera or materials.
- The free 512px tier may be too small for large hero art or print. Check the exact pack page for its download resolution and price.
- Commercial+ allows the image as part of a new design, but not as a standalone asset or competing asset library. The [licence agreement](https://shapefest.com/license-agreement) describes the conditions for commercial templates and UI kits.

## Reusable ideas

- Keep lighting, camera angle, scale, and shadow consistent across a group of objects so unrelated illustrations still read as one system.
- Separate material families into clear collections; users can decide on a finish before searching for a subject.
- Use black negative space and saturated edge lighting to make neon glass forms legible without adding interface chrome around them.
