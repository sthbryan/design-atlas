# Firefly Builds

## What it is

Firefly Builds is the portfolio of developer Jahnvi. Its light, spacious home page introduces the builder with a large serif name, a small portrait and an “Explore the site” command-palette prompt, then separates serious projects from fun experiments.

## When to open it

Open it when you want a compact student-developer portfolio that adds playful utility controls without hiding its projects, or when you need a reference for grouping exploratory work separately from production-oriented projects.

## Most useful

- **Pull-cord detail**: a fine line and hanging knob appear at the upper right, with a small “pull the cord!” hint that gives the otherwise minimal page a playful affordance.
- **Command palette entry**: search is presented as a visible invitation with its keyboard shortcut, as well as a top-navigation icon.
- **Project grouping**: the portfolio distinguishes “Serious Projects” from “Fun Projects,” letting practical work and creative experiments coexist without flattening them into one list.
- **Quiet typography**: a tall italic serif name anchors the hero while navigation and supporting labels use restrained small text.
- **Theme toggle**: a clear control in the header exposes a dark mode option.

## Using it with agents

No agent-facing integration was visible during the review. Navigation labels and project descriptions were readable in the browser; the palette, theme toggle and project destinations were not exercised.

## Watch out for

- The GitHub activity section reported that live data was unavailable in the initial view, then populated later during the review. Treat that activity panel as a live third-party data surface.
- The pull-cord interaction and mobile layout were not verified.
- No reuse licence was found for the site or its images; treat it as look-only.

## Reusable ideas

- Put the keyboard shortcut next to a command-palette invitation so the interaction is discoverable to both mouse and keyboard users.
- Separate polished work from experiments with clear labels rather than forcing one visual treatment for every project.
- Add a small physical-world motif to an otherwise restrained layout to give visitors a memorable interaction to look for.
