# Locomotive

## What it is

Locomotive is a Montreal digital design studio. Its portfolio moves from an audiovisual homepage to a filterable work archive and individual project case studies. The archive groups projects by service area and links out to selected client sites.

## When to open it

- When you need a reference for an agency portfolio that makes its work easy to browse while giving each project its own presentation.
- When studying how large editorial typography, imagery and motion can establish a strong art direction across a homepage, index and project detail page.

## Most useful

- The [homepage](https://locomotive.ca/en) opens with a full-bleed audiovisual hero. A single-line top navigation sits over it, alongside a very large serif headline and a compact OPS/DES/DEV studio mark.
- The [work archive](https://locomotive.ca/en/work) places projects in three text columns for name, sector and city on a white field. Filters sit above the list; expanding a row reveals project imagery, service tags, a short summary and links to its case study or live site.
- The [Truck'n Roll case study](https://locomotive.ca/en/work/truck-n-roll) changes scale with a mostly white hero: oversized serif title and project metadata sit beside a large photograph.
- The linked [Truck'n Roll site](https://trucknroll.com/) carries its own distinct art direction: black background, oversized slanted white logotype, a truck photograph and a bold sans-serif headline.

## Using it with agents

No published MCP, API, CLI, registry or agent guide was found. Give an agent the homepage, work archive or a specific case study URL and ask it to describe the visible hierarchy, image treatment and transitions between those page types.

## Watch out for

- The homepage and project pages rely on large media and strong typography; use the layout and pacing as a reference, then adapt the scale to the actual content and viewport.
- The studio's portfolio imagery and the client work shown in its case studies have no reuse licence stated on the reviewed pages. Treat them as look-only and check the client site separately before reusing anything.
- Motion is part of the presentation, but the accessible experience should still communicate each project when media is unavailable or reduced motion is enabled.

## Reusable ideas

- Put a work archive in a typographic table-like grid, then reveal detail in place so a visitor can scan the collection without losing context.
- Keep the project identity, metadata and key image visible together at the top of a case study.
- Let each client project keep its own visual language while using consistent archive and case-study navigation around it.
