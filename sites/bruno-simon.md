# Bruno Simon

## What it is

Bruno Simon's portfolio presents work and personal details inside a small 3D driving world. Visitors start at a night scene with a vehicle, then use keyboard or touch controls to move around and open information panels.

## When to open it

Open it when you are exploring how a portfolio can make navigation itself part of the visual concept, or how a 3D scene can hold both playful exploration and practical project information.

## Most useful

- **Landing scene**: a blue-violet grid recedes beneath a glowing circular platform, framing a compact vehicle, a lit streetlamp and a tree. The scene gives a clear focal point before the visitor starts.
- **Driving controls**: the start panel explains keyboard, mouse, touch and gamepad controls. Movement, camera changes and interactions each have dedicated inputs.
- **Information panels**: the home panel and options panel appear over the scene, preserving the world as context while making explanatory content readable.
- **Project discovery**: the world contains separate project areas, so exploring the portfolio doubles as spatial navigation.

## Using it with agents

There is no published agent interface or `llms.txt`. The public [folio-2025 source repository](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025) is available under MIT; use it when an agent needs to inspect implementation details rather than infer them from the live site.

## Watch out for

- The 3D world needs time to load and browser graphics support. Offer a direct, conventional route to important portfolio information.
- Navigation relies on game controls, so explain inputs clearly and provide an accessible alternative to driving.
- The repository gives its music a separate CC0 licence. Check asset-level rights before reusing artwork, models or other media.

## Reusable ideas

- Let a single visual metaphor connect navigation, content and interaction instead of treating them as separate layers.
- Teach the controls before asking visitors to explore, and keep the instructions available in a dedicated options panel.
- Use a small, sharply lit focal scene against a dark spatial backdrop to establish the experience's mood immediately.
