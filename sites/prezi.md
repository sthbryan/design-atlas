# Prezi

## What it is

Prezi's marketing page is a lesson in reordering a pitch. Instead of leading with the editor, it leads with the room: one QR code lets everyone join from a phone, upvote questions into a moderation queue, and answer polls and quizzes as the presenter reaches them. The layout follows that story in bands — "made to stick", "every voice", "all in one place", "after the session" — so a feature list becomes a narrative about what happens before, during and after a talk.

## When to open it

Open it when you are writing the page for an event, a workshop or a live product, and the interesting part is participation rather than the tool. The per-question results panel is the section worth studying: three questions, three percentages, one flagged as "needs another pass".

## Most useful

- **Participation framing**: joining without an app or account, remote attendees counted as present, and a moderation queue that keeps the presenter in control.
- **Results by question** (92%, 41%, 78%) with a short annotation, which is a better proof device than a satisfaction score alone.
- **A stated import path** from PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva and PDF, so the reader knows their existing deck survives.
- **One brand setup**: logo and colours added once, carried into everything made.
- **The after-session block**: the same link serves the full presentation, a transcript, flashcards and self-tests, which reframes the artefact as a place to return to.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, MCP server or API was found at review. The page is readable as text, so an agent can lift the band structure, but the live activities sit behind an account. It is a human reference for page structure, not a design source an agent can query.

## Watch out for

- Presentation tools are crowded and similar; the page's differentiation is participation, which will not apply to every project.
- The badge row (G2 leader categories) is social proof, not a design claim.
- Free tier limits, and prices for the paid tiers, are not visible on the pages reviewed.
- The story depends on an audience with phones; a one-to-one deck gains little.
- "Made to stick" language is marketing, and the typography on the page is conventional rather than instructive.

## Reusable ideas

- Structure a product page around the before, during and after of one real event.
- Show a results table with a failing number visible; it reads as honest measurement.
- Say how existing files come in, by name, near the top.
- Turn the output into something the audience can revisit rather than a file they download.
