# Design Minis

## What it is

Design Minis is a directory of small web tools made by designers, each doing one narrow job: a gradient builder, an easing editor, a halftone or dither effect, a font playground. It is run by one person, Kosta Motresku, under Belgian law according to its terms, and built with Next.js and Sanity on Vercel. Makers can submit their own tools, and a small set of the tools listed are built by the site itself.

## When to open it

- When you need a one-off graphic treatment (glitch, refraction, shattered glass, dithering, mirror tiling) for a hero, poster or social image and don't want a heavy app.
- When you want a small colour, type, easing or CSS helper and would rather browse a short curated list than search.
- When you need to cut out a background or trace a logo to SVG without uploading the image anywhere.

## Most useful

- **Catalogue**: 128 tools at review, added between February and September 2026. By pricing: 102 free, 23 freemium, 3 paid.
- **Categories** (up to three per tool): Motion 19, Images 16, Backgrounds 15, Effects 14, 3D 12, Color 12, Typography 10, Illustration 9, SVG 9, Icons 5, and a few in CSS, Video, Layout, Accessibility and Whiteboard.
- **In-house tools** (nine at review), which run on your device: Cutout (background removal with an on-device model), Trace (bitmap logo to SVG paths), Plinth (screenshot on a perspective backdrop), Refract, Shatter, Signal, Mirrors, Paper Studio and Hatchery.
- Every listing credits the tool's maker by name, usually with a link.

## Using it with agents

No llms.txt, MCP or API. Use it by hand: make the asset in a tool, export PNG, SVG or video, commit it, and point the agent at the file. Trace's SVG output is the most useful to an agent, because the agent can edit the markup directly. The terms ask visitors not to scrape the directory wholesale.

## Watch out for

- One sponsored slot is mixed into the grid (an AI media service at review) and labelled "Sponsored"; the terms say listings are not endorsements.
- Listed tools set their own privacy terms; the site's no-upload statement covers only its nine in-house tools.
- Its terms warn the in-house tools may break when browsers change, so keep your source files.
- Tracing or cutting out a logo or photo doesn't change who owns it, as its terms point out.

## Reusable ideas

- Putting the maker's name on every listing turns a directory into credit for independent tool-builders.
- A short privacy page that says what it doesn't collect is clearer than a long policy.
- Letting a tool sit in up to three categories helps where effects, images and backgrounds overlap.
