# Tally

## What it is

A form builder that argues in the negative. The headline says "the simplest way to create forms", the paragraph says goodbye to boring forms and surveys, and the call to action promises a free form with no signup required. The layout is one column on white, a single sans-serif at two or three sizes, no illustrations and no device frames: forms are shown as forms.

The product claim underneath is the design decision worth noting. "Just start typing" — the editor works like a text document, with blocks inserted the way a writing app does, rather than like a canvas you drag fields onto. That is a different mental model from every drag-and-drop builder, and it is why the marketing site can look so plain: the thing being sold is the absence of interface.

## When to open it

Open it when you are designing a form and want a reference for restraint, or when you need to argue that a data-entry tool should feel like writing rather than configuring. Also useful as an example of a utility product site that does not decorate its utility.

## Most useful

- **The document metaphor stated plainly**, in a product category full of canvases and grids.
- **A free form before signup**, the cleanest possible proof for a builder.
- **Social proof as one line** — 2,500,000+ teams at review — instead of a logo wall.
- **Privacy treated as a feature with specifics** (GDPR, hosted in Europe, no cookie tracking, encryption in transit and at rest) rather than a badge.
- **Unlimited free usage with published fair-usage limits**, which is more honest than an invisible cap.

## Using it with agents

`llms.txt` is served at review, so an agent can read plans, limits and feature pages as text. No public REST API or MCP server was found on the page read; forms are built in the editor.

## Watch out for

- A proprietary product, so the free tier is generosity rather than a licence.
- Unlimited usage is bounded by fair-use guidelines; read them before building a workflow on it.
- The plain aesthetic is the product choice, not a general template: a marketing site needs range that a form builder does not.
- Form accessibility — error announcements, grouping, required-field semantics — is not demonstrated on the marketing page.

## Reusable ideas

- Sell the absence of interface when that is the feature.
- Let a visitor produce something real before asking for an account.
- Replace a logo wall with one measured number.
- Make privacy claims concrete: where the data lives, what is not tracked, what is encrypted.
