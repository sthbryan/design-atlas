# 100,000 Stars

## What it is

100,000 Stars is an interactive astronomy visualization from the Chrome Experiments collection. A dark, nearly full-screen star field places the dense Milky Way around a bright center point; visitors can move through the field and explore stellar scale. Minimal playback and chart controls sit in the upper-left corner, leaving most of the viewport for the visualization.

## When to open it

Open it when you are designing an immersive data view, spatial visualization or cinematic introduction to a large dataset. It is useful for studying how scale and navigation can be communicated through position, density, light and movement instead of a dashboard full of panels.

## Most useful

- **Star field**: thousands of small, varied points create depth against black, with a brighter central cluster as the first visual anchor.
- **Navigation**: movement through the scene turns a static overview into an exploration of relative position and scale.
- **Playback and chart controls**: small controls stay at the edge while the visualization fills the page.
- **Controls**: the play and chart icons stay tucked into the upper-left corner, keeping the star field visually dominant.

## Using it with agents

There is no published agent integration. Share the URL and name the visual question to study, such as how an overview transitions into a close-up. No project-specific reuse licence was found for the experience, so treat it as a visual reference rather than source code or asset material.

## Watch out for

- This is a focused scientific visualization, not a general-purpose interface example.
- Full-screen 3D navigation can be disorienting or difficult on touch devices; provide a clear overview and conventional controls in related projects.
- The experience depends on browser graphics support and may take time to load its stellar data.

## Reusable ideas

- Use a single visual anchor to orient people before inviting them to explore a large space.
- Encode density and distance through scale, position and restrained light rather than adding labels everywhere.
- Keep controls small and persistent at the edge of an immersive canvas.
