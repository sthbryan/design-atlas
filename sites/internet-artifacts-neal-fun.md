# Internet Artifacts

## What it is

Internet Artifacts is an interactive Neal.fun project that presents notable objects and moments from early internet history along a timeline. The entry screen frames the experience like a museum display, with a parchment-colored field, illustrated side columns and a centered title plaque. Entering leads to individual artifact pages with period imagery, long-form notes and previous/next navigation.

## When to open it

Open it when you are exploring a playful archive, interactive history page or editorial project that should feel like entering a designed world. It is a reference for visual framing and navigation across a sequence, rather than a chart library or component source.

## Most useful

- **Entry screen**: a quiet central title panel and a row of hand-drawn internet characters establish the archive's tone before the reader starts.
- **Year navigation**: the year labels imply a long timeline while keeping the entry action prominent.
- **Artifact pages**: each item pairs a large historic visual with contextual writing; Prev and Next Artifact keep exploration continuous.
- **Catalog**: provides a route back to the list when readers want to jump rather than move sequentially.

## Using it with agents

There is no published agent integration. Share the project URL and describe its timeline, illustrated framing or sequential navigation as the reference. Its text and artwork have no general reuse licence stated, so do not ask an agent to reproduce them.

## Watch out for

- The archive has a specific nostalgic theme; its parchment treatment and vintage imagery will not suit every subject.
- The page is interaction-led and contains substantial explanatory text, so decide how the experience should work for keyboard and reduced-motion users before borrowing its pacing.
- Verify historical claims and media rights independently before building a similar archive.

## Reusable ideas

- Give a timeline a strong first frame that establishes the subject and offers a clear way in.
- Keep sequential navigation visible while preserving a catalog route for readers who want to jump.
- Pair each artifact with a short visual anchor and enough context to explain its place in the larger sequence.
