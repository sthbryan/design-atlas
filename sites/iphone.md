# iPhone

## What it is

Apple’s live iPhone product overview, which links the current model lineup to product pages, comparison and shopping. The page uses product imagery, feature galleries and editorial sections to guide a long scroll through the range.

## When to open it

Use the [iPhone page](https://www.apple.com/iphone/) to study how a product family is presented on a current commercial site. The page changes as Apple updates its lineup; the products and images observed here reflect the 2026 review date.

## Most useful

- The opening pairs a large, plain “iPhone” heading with a horizontal row of compact model images and names. A broad product card follows, with a short title, one-line descriptor, and clear learn-more and pricing actions.
- Product cards use generous whitespace, pale neutral fields and large isolated device photography. Color accents appear on actions and on the device finishes, letting the product images provide most of the palette.
- Lower feature modules divide the story into subjects such as design, camera, chip and privacy. Their repeated heading, concise text and product image create a consistent rhythm through the long page.
- The model strip and feature areas expose carousel controls; the hero gallery has a play control. The visible page offers movement and selection, although this review did not characterize its transition timing or easing.

## Using it with agents

No public MCP, API, CLI, skill or agent guide was found. Apple’s `/llms.txt` path returned 404 at review. An agent can inspect the public page and its galleries in a browser, but product photography and site imagery are Apple content rather than reusable assets.

## Watch out for

- This is a product and shopping page, not a neutral product comparison. Pricing and model availability can vary by country and change after review.
- Apple’s website terms reserve the content and restrict copying or redistribution. Do not reuse the imagery, product renders, Apple logo or visual interface; adapt only the broad composition ideas in original work.

## Reusable ideas

- Give a product family a compact horizontal index, then let a large visual card introduce one model at a time.
- Break a long product page into repeatable feature sections with one topic, one concise explanation and one image.
- Keep commercial actions visible beside the relevant product summary instead of burying them in a general footer.
