# Supermassive Games

## What it is

Supermassive Games' homepage is a cinematic introduction to the narrative game studio. A dark header with social links frames a full-width cast image, overlaid by a large headline and a route to its games. The [games catalogue](https://www.supermassivegames.com/games) organizes recent and older titles into an image-led list; the [Directive 8020 page](https://www.supermassivegames.com/games/directive8020) gives one title a large atmospheric hero followed by platform, premise and official-site links.

## When to open it

Open it to study a game studio homepage built around a cinematic hero, or a catalogue that connects a consistent studio shell to distinct game identities.

## Most useful

- **Homepage hero**: a full-width image of the cast establishes genre and mood; a centered message and Our Games action sit over the scene.
- **Persistent frame**: a compact logo and social row share the dark header with Games, Careers, News and About.
- **Catalogue**: game entries pair large key art with title, year and platforms, while previous titles are separated into their own group.
- **Dedicated title page**: Directive 8020 keeps an atmospheric image behind its title and platform line, then places the synopsis and official destination below.

## Using it with agents

No published agent channel was found. Ask an agent to compare the homepage hero, games index and a title page for overlay contrast, catalogue grouping and how much information appears before an outbound store or game link. The footer says all rights are reserved; game art, video and marks are look-only.

## Watch out for

- The page relies on large game imagery and a cookie-control panel; check that headline and CTA remain readable against the current hero frame.
- Some trailer embeds appeared blank in the browser inspection, while page text and game key art loaded. Recheck embedded media in the target browser.
- The site's games link to publisher or official title pages; current prices and terms may differ by platform.

## Reusable ideas

- Put one clear action over the hero image and keep its contrast stable across image changes.
- Group a long catalogue by release recency and title history so the newest work is easy to find.
- Preserve one studio navigation frame while allowing each game page to use its own art direction.
