# Launch Videos Gallery

## What it is

A gallery of product launch videos, hand-picked from X and posted daily, each entry credited to the account that made it. At review the feed mixed AI, Consumer and Development launches: Replit, Figma, Runway and Google beside small accounts with a few hundred views. Every card carries the same compact set of numbers — view count, replies, reposts, likes, and how many hours ago it appeared — so the page doubles as a live read on which launch format is getting watched this week.

## When to open it

Open it when you are planning the 10-second clip that sells a product and want to see what people actually ship, rather than a case study written after the fact. It is most useful read top-down: the newest entries show current fashion, and the outliers with 100× the views of their neighbours show the format that worked.

## Most useful

- **Recency as the sort**: five hours to two days, no archive dig. That keeps the reference current in a category that dates fast.
- **Category chips** (AI, Consumer, Development, with a "+2" for the rest) narrow the feed to the audience you are designing for.
- **Engagement visible per card**, so you can compare a 1.9K-view clip against a 537K one without opening either.
- **The creator's handle**, which is the actual reference: follow the person, not the site.
- **Email digest** for people who do not want to check a feed daily.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, API or export was found at review. The feed is server-rendered text, so an agent can read titles, categories and counts, but the videos are X embeds and cannot be analysed from the page. A useful division is for a person to shortlist clips and the agent to write the shot list from what they describe.

## Watch out for

- Every clip is somebody's work under their own rights, posted publicly. Watching for reference is not a licence to reuse footage, music or motion.
- View counts on X move with the account's reach, not with the quality of the motion.
- The feed is one curator's taste plus X's momentum, so popular formats crowd out quieter ones.
- Cards show counts and categories rather than a description, so you must play the clip to know what it is.
- "Updated daily" is a claim about the feed, not an archive; older entries scroll away.

## Reusable ideas

- Put the metric you want people to sort by on the card itself, not behind a click.
- Tag by audience or vertical, not by tool, when the reference is about an outcome.
- Show time-since-post on every item in a fast-moving gallery; it is the cheapest freshness label.
- Keep the curation visible by naming the account behind each item.
