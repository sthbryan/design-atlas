# Typeform

## What it is

A form platform, and the first thing a visitor meets is not a form: it is a banner offering to connect Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP-ready assistant to the account. Typeform MCP appears in the platform navigation marked New, beside Typeform AI (described as an AI know-pilot, a pun that tells you where the marketing effort is), Growth Flow for automated workflows and Research Flow for AI-moderated studies.

What is still visible from the original product is the compositional idea that made the brand: one question at a time, a lot of empty space, a large question and a single input. Their own site keeps that register — generous vertical rhythm, big type, one idea per screen — even when the subject is enterprise automation.

## When to open it

Open it when you are designing a multi-step form or onboarding flow and want the reference for one question per screen, or when you want to see how an established product site absorbs an AI feature without abandoning its visual identity. Also worth reading as a case of announcing an integration in the top banner rather than in a changelog.

## Most useful

- **One question per step**, the pattern most multi-step flows still copy badly, with the spacing and type sizes that make it work.
- **A persistent announcement bar for the MCP integration**, which treats an agent channel as a headline feature.
- **Product names that describe a workflow** — research, growth — rather than a feature list.
- **A navigation that separates platform, AI and integrations**, so the AI story does not swallow the product.

## Using it with agents

The strongest agent story in this batch after the design systems: Typeform publishes an MCP server, states that assistants can connect to an account, and serves `llms.txt` at review. An agent can therefore read the site as text and act on forms through the MCP server rather than scraping.

## Watch out for

- The AI layer is young and its naming is marketing-first; check what the MCP server can actually do before designing a workflow around it.
- Pricing is proprietary and tiered; the free tier is a trial in practice.
- The one-question-at-a-time pattern is expensive in clicks for long forms and hostile to comparison; it suits conversational intake, not applications.
- The site is heavy with navigation; the calm of the form is a product artefact, not the site's structure.

## Reusable ideas

- Put an agent integration in the top banner when it is genuinely new.
- Keep the visual register of the product you are known for, even when selling automation.
- Name products after the job — research, growth — instead of the technology.
- One idea per screen, with space around it; the pattern survives being copied.
