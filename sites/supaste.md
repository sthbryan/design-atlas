# Supaste

## What it is

Supaste is a macOS clipboard-history app. Its product page uses a bright blue gradient hero and a simulated notch shelf to show a searchable visual history with categories for text, colors, assets and inspirations. The site also walks through the app's library, filters and quick-paste features.

## When to open it

Open the [home page](https://supaste.com/) when designing a landing page for a native Mac utility or explaining a dense productivity app through product mockups. Scroll through the feature sections to see the notch shelf, library, custom categories and content filters shown in context.

## Most useful

- The hero connects a short two-line promise to a detailed, interactive-looking app mockup rather than relying on an abstract illustration.
- The vivid blue field and white display type give the first screen a clear focal point; a contrasting serif line adds emphasis without taking over the hierarchy.
- Feature sections alternate specific product views with concise explanations, revealing the same app through progressively deeper workflows.
- Product benefits such as search, type filters, OCR and custom categories are shown as controls in the mockup, helping explain what the app does.

## Using it with agents

The site has no public agent integration found at review. Use it as a reference for presenting a macOS utility and its visual states; the app itself is a paid download.

## Watch out for

- The app is proprietary and licensed per device. The marketing mockup and screenshots are not licensed UI assets for reuse.
- The site's $15 early-user offer and limited-seat count can change; verify the current price before purchasing.
- Clipboard history is a macOS-specific product pattern; adapt the notch shelf to the actual platform rather than copying the hardware metaphor into unrelated contexts.

## Reusable ideas

- Show a recognizable product surface in the hero and use it to explain the core interaction immediately.
- Organize a long feature page by user task, then pair each task with a focused product view.
- Demonstrate content types with distinct visual treatments while keeping them inside one searchable library.
- Use short utility copy to connect a large typographic headline with a more detailed product mockup.
