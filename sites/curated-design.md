# Curated

## What it is

Curated is a gallery of live websites, each reviewed by a person and filed by industry and visual style, with every record linking to the real page. It is run by the team behind [Craftwork](craftwork.md). Its changelog says it began on Webflow in 2022, spent about eleven months as a section of craftwork.design, and returned to its own domain in August 2026. Alongside the site gallery it keeps a section library of page blocks such as heroes, pricing tables, footers and FAQs.

## When to open it

- When you know the look you want and need live examples in that style. The [Neobrutalism shelf](https://curated.design/inspiration/neobrutalism/) is a direct starting point for brutalist and neobrutalist references.
- When you want to compare many versions of one section type, and a Pro trial is acceptable.
- When you want references you can open at full size, since every entry is a working site.

## Most useful

- The [style and industry shelves](https://curated.design/inspiration/) combine visual direction with the kind of site. The neobrutalism shelf currently shows 83 records, including agency, portfolio, product and finance examples.
- Individual records show their category and style tags, a screenshot, and a **Live at** link. For example, [Basement Studio](https://curated.design/sites/s/44841/) is tagged Neobrutalism, Animated, Colorful and Dark and links to [basement.studio](https://basement.studio/); [Tinloof](https://curated.design/sites/s/44837/) is tagged Neobrutalism and Minimal and links to [tinloof.com](https://tinloof.com/).
- The separate [section library](https://curated.design/sections/) groups examples by type and links each section to the page it came from. A sample is free; the full library is part of Pro.
- Browsing the full website collection is free. Pro is $9/month with a seven-day trial; it adds the full section library, unlimited bookmarks, advanced filters and CSV exports.

## Using it with agents

No MCP, llms.txt or API is listed. For a visual request, open the matching shelf, choose two or three records, then follow **Live at** to the real sites. Inspect them in the browser at desktop and mobile widths; compare the page structure, typography, spacing, colour, borders and interaction, and tell the agent which specific decisions to adapt. For instance, start from the [neobrutalism shelf](https://curated.design/inspiration/neobrutalism/) and compare [Basement Studio](https://basement.studio/) with [Tinloof](https://tinloof.com/) rather than treating the style label as the whole brief. Pro's CSV export can keep a reference list with a project brief.

## Watch out for

- The whole website collection is browsable free, but only a sample of each section type is free; the full library requires Pro. A Pro account is for one person.
- The page also sells Framer templates from Craftwork's store, a paid "skip the queue" review ($29) and $149-a-week sponsor cards, so check what's editorial and what's paid.
- Submissions go through Craftwork's review queue, so the catalogue reflects one studio's taste.
- Style tags are a discovery aid. Open the live site and check its current design before treating an old screenshot or label as a usable reference.

## Reusable ideas

- Two independent axes, what a site is for and what it looks like, let people find "dark fintech" or "pastel portfolio" without a single tag cloud.
- Taking pages apart into sections and lining up forty of one type shows the conventions an industry has settled on.
- Charging for review speed and feedback, not for acceptance, keeps a paid submission path honest.
