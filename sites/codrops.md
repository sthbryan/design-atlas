# Codrops

## What it is

Codrops publishes creative frontend tutorials and live demos, alongside a Creative Hub of original and selected experiments and Webzibition, a curated showcase of notable websites. Its work ranges from CSS interaction details to Three.js, WebGL, typography and full-page experiences. The demo library is the most direct route to runnable examples; tutorials often explain how an effect was built.

## When to open it

Open it when a page needs a distinctive interaction or visual technique and you want to inspect both the result and an implementation. The Creative Hub is useful for browsing experiments; Webzibition and case studies are better for studying complete art direction and composition.

## Most useful

- **Demos Hub**: downloadable prototypes tagged with techniques such as Three.js, shaders, cursor trails and typography. Open a demo before borrowing its visual move.
- **Creative Hub**: a mix of Codrops originals and selected live creative-web experiments.
- **Webzibition**: curated full websites, useful for seeing how expressive ideas work as a complete page.
- **Tutorials and case studies**: technical walkthroughs and design context for selected effects.
- **Motion highlights**: a topic route for interaction and animation roundups.

## Using it with agents

There is no published agent integration. Give an agent a specific demo or tutorial URL and describe the visual behavior you want to study. The downloadable demos are MIT licensed unless a particular demo states otherwise; verify any included font, image or other third-party asset separately.

## Watch out for

- The gallery spans experiments rather than a consistent production-ready design system.
- A spectacular demo may use heavy WebGL or motion that is unsuitable for a normal product interface.
- Codrops notes separate terms for design freebies and article text; the demo license does not grant rights to featured sites or every bundled asset.

## Reusable ideas

- Pair an effect demo with its implementation notes so the visual result and technical method can be inspected together.
- Use topic tags to narrow experimental work by technique, then compare a few examples before settling on a direction.
- Separate curated full-site references from small interaction experiments so each is browsed at the right scale.
