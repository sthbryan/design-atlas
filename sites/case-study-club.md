# Case Study Club

## What it is

Case Study Club is a weekly design publication presented as a long-form web issue, with a dispatch, field notes, a featured portfolio, an archive story and a design tool. The homepage also collects past issues and links to the featured designers' work.

## When to open it

- When you need a quiet editorial layout for a design newsletter or a long page with several distinct story types.
- When studying how typography and small navigation details can carry a restrained publication without decorative imagery in the opening panel.

## Most useful

- The opening subscribe panel is a centered, off-white sheet against a pale gray page, framed by thin black rules. A large, heavy sans-serif headline leads; small uppercase labels with wide tracking mark the masthead and footer.
- Below the subscription panel, the current issue uses a narrow editorial column and recurring section labels to separate a lead story, short links, a portfolio feature and an archive item.
- The issue archive at [the homepage](https://www.casestudy.club/) links directly to individual issues, such as [issue 017](https://www.casestudy.club/issues/017/).
- A featured portfolio appears in the issue itself; the current example links to [Buzz Usborne](https://buzzusborne.com/) and his [work page](https://buzzusborne.com/work/).

## Using it with agents

There is no published MCP, API, CLI, registry or agent guide. Use the live issue as a visual reference and follow its outbound links to inspect an individual designer's work.

## Watch out for

- This is a publication, not a searchable gallery of interface screenshots or reusable design assets.
- Its simple opening panel is a stronger reference for editorial hierarchy, type scale and rules than for interaction or motion.
- No reuse licence for the writing or imagery is stated; use the site for visual study only.

## Reusable ideas

- Put a publication's masthead, issue rhythm and signup action on one calm, paper-like panel, then continue into the editorial content below.
- Use recurring uppercase section markers and hairline rules to make a long issue scannable without adding heavy card chrome.
- Give lead stories room for a large title and readable paragraphs, while keeping peripheral notes and links compact.
