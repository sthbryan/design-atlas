# Font In Logo

## What it is

Font In Logo answers a narrow question well: which typeface is that logo set in? It keeps a searchable database of famous marks — Nike, Tesla, Chanel, Adidas, YouTube, BMW, Sony, Nvidia and The New York Times are named on the home page — and gives each entry a confidence label. The labels are the useful part: **Verified** means its designers checked, **Based On** and **Modified** describe derivatives of a known face, **The Closest Font** is an approximation for marks set in custom lettering, **Community Suggestion** is unverified, and **Community Designed** marks a fan-made lookalike. It also browses film, TV, anime and game titles, where the answer is usually a free display face, and offers a logo text generator. The site grew out of the design team at Azwedo, which also sells Webflow templates promoted between entries.

## When to open it

Open it when a brief names a brand's look and you need to know whether the type is a licensable family, a custom drawing or a free lookalike — a distinction that decides what you can actually use. It is also a quick source of free display faces for a title treatment.

## Most useful

- **Confidence labels** on every identification, which is rare in this genre and worth reading before you trust an entry.
- **Film and game titles** sit beside corporate logos, and those entries name free fonts such as Anton, Pacifico, Orbitron and Press Start 2P, so the reference is directly usable.
- **Modified and custom cases** are recorded as such rather than forced into a family name, which is exactly the case designers get wrong.
- **Community submissions** open the database to correction and to obscure marks.
- **Logo text generator** turns an identification into a quick set-type preview.

## Using it with agents

No `llms.txt`, API, MCP server or export was found at review. Results are long pages of cards, and the text is readable enough for an agent to search, but there is no structured endpoint, so an agent should quote the label with any answer it gives. The honest pattern is: human searches, agent reads the specific entry page.

## Watch out for

- An identification is an editorial claim, not a legal fact. Community suggestions are unverified, and closest-match entries are approximations by definition.
- Even a verified family is usually a commercial licence. Free display faces on film and title entries are the ones you can ship.
- The site's own name misspells itself as "Foninlogo" on the about page, and some promo blocks sit between results, which makes the results harder to scan.
- Promoted Webflow templates are interleaved with identifications; they are ads, not references.
- Logos are trademarks. Knowing the font does not grant the right to imitate a mark.

## Reusable ideas

- Grade every entry by how certain it is, with named categories rather than a single vague "approximate".
- Record derivatives honestly: "based on" and "modified" are more useful than naming a face the logo does not use.
- Put a short, direct answer on every card, so the page can be scanned without opening each entry.
- Let the community submit and let designers verify, and keep the two visibly different.
