# Duolingo Art Style

## What it is

An illustrated 2020 article by Duolingo artists explaining the evolution and working principles behind the app’s art style. It shows vector illustrations, UI placements, character examples and a before-and-after comparison of skill icons.

## When to open it

Open the article when you need to make small illustrations legible at a glance while giving them a distinct, expressive personality. The examples connect shape choices and composition to compact app screens.

## Most useful

- **Shape reduction**: the cat-shape example and icon comparisons show how a few large, clear vector forms can replace fine surface detail.
- **Silhouette and spacing**: the article gives illustrations white space so their primary forms stay distinct from surrounding interface content.
- **Character and story**: exaggerated proportions, props and gesture make simple vocabulary scenes more memorable without changing the learning prompt.
- **Attention direction**: angled lines and movement in skill icons lead the eye toward the adjacent action or lesson.

## Using it with agents

No public MCP, API, CLI, `llms.txt` or agent guide was linked from the reviewed article. An agent can inspect the article images in a browser and use them to discuss shape economy, silhouettes and visual hierarchy. The illustrations and characters are copyrighted; create original artwork rather than reusing them.

## Watch out for

- The article dates from 2020 and describes the style as it stood then. Duolingo links separate brand guidelines from its current site; check those before treating these examples as current production rules.
- The examples are specific to an education product and its characters. Transfer the clarity and composition principles, not the owl or the exact brand palette.
- The article explains design goals as well as showing the work. Judge the composition from the illustrations themselves rather than repeating business or learning outcome claims.

## Reusable ideas

- Start with the smallest set of shapes that communicates the subject, then test whether its silhouette reads at small sizes.
- Use negative space to separate an illustration from nearby interface controls and text.
- Build personality through proportion, gesture and a small story instead of adding decorative detail everywhere.
- Direct attention with a purposeful angle or motion cue that points toward the next relevant UI element.
