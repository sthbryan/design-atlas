# Ghost

## What it is

Ghost is an open source publishing platform for creators and newsletters, with Ghost(Pro) as the managed hosting option. The pricing page is a good study in pricing for an audience business: the plan choice follows the size of the member list, because for a creator audience, that is the growth metric that matters.

## When to open it

- When a publishing product prices by audience size, and the pricing page should let visitors see their own growth in the price.
- When an open source core with a paid hosted tier needs a pricing page that respects self-hosters instead of hiding them.
- When creator discounts (education, non-profit) are part of the offer and should be visible, not discovered by asking support.

## Most useful

- A member-count control ties the price to the creator's actual audience, making the growth-based model tangible before signup.
- The education discount is surfaced on the pricing page itself, which treats the discount as part of the offer rather than a secret.
- The split between open source and Ghost(Pro) is stated plainly, so self-hosters know the product is real and hosted users know what they pay for.
- The site's editorial tone carries through to product pages, which suits a platform whose users are writers.

## Using it with agents

- No agent channel is declared on the site at review (no MCP, llms.txt or CLI linked from the docs hub at review).
- Ghost has an Admin API, but it needs credentials; an agent can only use the public site and docs without an account.

## Watch out for

- Ghost(Pro) plans are priced by audience size, so re-check the pricing page when the subscriber count changes.
- The GPL licence applies to the code; publishing with Ghost does not license your content, but modifying and redistributing Ghost does trigger the GPL.
- Pricing figures at review are stated on the pricing page; confirm them live before quoting.

## Reusable ideas

- Price by the customer's growth metric (audience, traffic, seats) with a control that updates the plan live.
- Show discounts for students and non-profits as first-class pricing content, not as a footnote.
- Present an open source core and a paid hosted tier side by side, with an honest comparison, when both are real options.
